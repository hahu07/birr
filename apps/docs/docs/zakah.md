# Product Three: Zakah

## What this is

Zakah is the obligatory alms a Muslim pays each year on wealth they've
held for a full lunar year, once it's above a threshold called the
**nisab**. It isn't charity in the general sense: it may only go to the
eight groups of people named in the Qur'an (9:60).

Birr offers two things for zakah:

1. **A free calculator** that works out what you owe, in Naira, and
   shows you exactly how it got there.
2. **A way to pay it through Birr**, which then delivers it to eligible
   people on your behalf.

Zakah is different from Birr's other two products. With a
[Waqf Fund](./waqf-funds) or a [Vault](./vaults), Birr is a
**trustee** holding an endowment or a campaign's money. With zakah,
Birr is your **agent** (in Arabic, *wakil*). The zakah is still yours
until it reaches a recipient. Birr only carries it there, in your name.
That's why zakah has its own records and its own bank account, kept
apart from everything else Birr holds.

|  | Waqf Fund | Vault | Zakah |
|---|---|---|---|
| What Birr is | Trustee | Trustee | Your agent |
| Whose money it is | The endowment's | The campaign's | Yours, until it reaches a recipient |
| Who can receive it | A Founder's chosen causes | A campaign's causes | Only the eight groups in Qur'an 9:60 |
| Does Birr keep any of it? | No | No | No. All of your zakah reaches recipients |

**Our example throughout this page: Amina**, who runs a fabric shop in
Kano. She has ₦3,000,000 in savings, ₦1,200,000 of stock in her shop,
and owes her supplier ₦400,000, due now. She's held this wealth for a
full lunar year.

## When it opens

Two separate approvals have to happen first, and the zakah page says
which one it's waiting for:

- **The calculator** opens once Birr's Shariah Supervisory Board has
  approved the rulings it applies, and today's gold and silver prices
  have been approved (they set the nisab).
- **Paying through Birr** opens once Birr's Legal Adviser and the
  Shariah Supervisory Board have approved the wording that appoints
  Birr as your agent. Until then the page says "Paying your zakah
  through Birr opens soon", and the calculator still works on its own.

## Working out your zakah

Open the zakah page on Birr's website and enter what you own and what
you owe.

1. **Choose your school of thought.** The four schools (Maliki, Hanafi,
   Shafi'i and Hanbali) differ on a few points, such as whether jewellery
   you wear is counted and how debts are treated. You can only choose a
   school whose rulings the Shariah Supervisory Board has approved.
2. **Enter your wealth.** Cash and savings, business stock (valued at
   today's selling price), money others owe you, and any gold or silver
   you own by weight and purity. Tick "I wear this as jewellery" for
   pieces you wear rather than keep as savings; your school decides
   whether those count.
3. **Enter debts due now.** Money you have to pay right away, such as
   Amina's ₦400,000 to her supplier.
4. **Confirm a full lunar year.** Zakah is only due on wealth you've
   held for a full lunar year, so the calculator asks you to confirm
   that before it shows a figure.

The calculator then compares your wealth with today's nisab and shows
the result, with a "How we worked it out" breakdown line by line.

**For Amina:**

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Savings | ₦3,000,000 |
| Shop stock | ₦1,200,000 |
| Debts due now | − ₦400,000 |
| **Wealth zakah is due on** | **₦3,800,000** |
| **Zakah due (2.5%)** | **₦95,000** |

₦95,000 is due **if** ₦3,800,000 is above the nisab. Whether it is
depends on a decision the Shariah Supervisory Board still has to make:
whether the nisab is measured against gold or silver. A gold-based
nisab is roughly ten times a silver-based one in Naira, so the same
₦3,800,000 can be well above one and below the other. The calculator
always uses the basis the Board has approved.

A few things to know:

- **Your figures stay on your device.** The calculator downloads the
  approved rulings and prices, then does the arithmetic in your
  browser. Birr never receives what you typed in.
- **Prices are checked for age.** The nisab moves with gold and silver
  prices, which Birr's staff set and a second person approves. If
  they're more than 7 days old, the calculator warns you. If they're
  more than 31 days old, it won't give a figure at all.
- **Zakah is rounded up**, to the kobo, in the recipients' favour.
- **What isn't covered yet:** livestock, crops, minerals, shares,
  pensions and crypto. Each has its own rules, and the calculator
  doesn't include them yet.

## Paying through Birr

Once paying is open, you can pay straight from your result.

1. **Read and accept the agency wording.** It appoints Birr as your
   agent to deliver your zakah only to people eligible for it, as
   determined by Birr's Shariah Supervisory Board. It also says Birr
   won't use your zakah for its own running costs, and that once your
   zakah has been delivered it can't be returned.
2. **Decide about the processing fee.** Paystack charges a fee on every
   payment. You can tick a box to add it on top. Either way, **all of
   your zakah reaches recipients**: if you don't add it, Birr pays the
   fee from its own funds. It is never taken out of your zakah.
3. **Add an email if you want a receipt.** It's optional. Without one,
   Birr can't send you a receipt.
4. **Pay with Paystack:** card, bank transfer or USSD.

**Amina** pays her ₦95,000 and ticks the fee box, which adds
₦1,548.23. She pays ₦96,548.23 in total, and the full ₦95,000 goes to
recipients.

- The smallest payment accepted is ₦100.
- For larger payments, you'll be asked for your full name and a form of
  ID (NIN, international passport or driver's licence). Birr is
  required to confirm who you are for payments of that size.
- After paying, a confirmation page updates by itself until your bank
  confirms. Bank transfers can take a little longer than cards.
- Your payment record keeps only the amount, plus which approved rulings
  and prices were in force, so the figure can always be explained later.
  It never stores the wealth you entered in the calculator.

## Where your zakah goes

Zakah may only go to the eight groups named in Qur'an 9:60:

1. The poor (*al-fuqara*)
2. The needy (*al-masakin*)
3. Zakah administrators (*al-'amilin*). **Birr never takes this
   share.** It's closed in Birr's systems, so all of your zakah goes to
   the other groups.
4. Those whose hearts are to be reconciled (*al-mu'allafah qulubuhum*)
5. Freeing those in bondage (*fi al-riqab*)
6. Those in debt (*al-gharimin*)
7. In the cause of Allah (*fi sabilillah*)
8. The stranded traveller (*ibn al-sabil*)

Birr delivers zakah in two ways:

- **To individuals Birr has verified as eligible.** Two different
  members of Birr's staff are involved: one registers the person, and a
  second approves them before they can be paid. Changing an approved
  person's bank details also needs a second person's approval.
- **Through vetted partner organizations**, for example a food bank.
  Partners have to report back on the people they reached.

Every payment out is approved by someone other than the person who
proposed it. It's paid by bank transfer from Birr's separate zakah
account, and only recorded as paid once another person has checked the
bank's reference. Birr can never commit more zakah than it has
received.

## Zakah al-Fitr

Zakah al-Fitr is the smaller zakah due from each person at the end of
Ramadan. In Birr's calculator it's one *sa'* of the season's staple
food for each person in your household, including children, valued in
Naira. Birr's staff set each season's staple and amount, and a member
of the Shariah Supervisory Board approves them before the season opens.

- **Enter how many people** you're paying for. The total is always
  worked out as people × that season's amount per person.
- **Choose food or cash.** Food is open to everyone. Cash, passed on as
  money, is only offered for those following the Hanafi school.
- **Payments close the day before Eid**, so there's time to deliver
  before the Eid prayer. If you miss it, give it directly to someone in
  need before the prayer.
- Zakah al-Fitr goes only to **the poor and the needy**.

If, say, this season's amount were ₦4,000 a person, Amina's household
of five would pay ₦20,000. The zakah page shows the actual amount for
the season.

## If something went wrong

Refunds are only for mistakes, such as paying twice, paying the wrong
amount, or a payment you didn't authorise. Zakah you've paid can't be
taken back because you've changed your mind, and once it's been
delivered it can't be returned at all.

To ask for a refund, contact Birr. A refund needs a second person's
approval, from Birr's Board of Trustees, and is paid by bank transfer
to your account. Only the zakah itself is refunded, not a processing fee
you chose to cover. If you gave an email, you'll be told when the refund
is approved and again when it's paid.
