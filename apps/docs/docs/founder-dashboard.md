# The Founder Dashboard (Plain-English Guide)

This page explains, in simple terms, what you see after you sign up
with Birr and set up your first waqf. No jargon, just a walkthrough —
with an example so it's easier to picture.

**Our example:** Amina runs a small charity called **Hope for Kids
Foundation**. She's set up two things through Birr:

1. **Scholarship Waqf** — she invested $50,000. The money itself stays
   invested and untouched forever; only the *profit* it earns each year
   pays for scholarships.
2. **Clean Water Project** — a one-time project to drill wells in three
   villages, funded by donations people give directly to it.

Once Amina finishes signing up, she sees a sidebar with six sections:
**Overview, Portfolio, Messages, Impact, Team, Activity** — plus an
**Account & security** page tucked under her name at the bottom.

One rule applies to the *entire* dashboard, so it's worth saying up
front: **Amina can look at everything and ask for things, but she can
never personally approve or make a "real" change herself.** Anything
that actually moves money or changes how a waqf is run — paying out to
someone, changing an investment, disposing of an asset — has to be
done by Birr's own staff, and always by *two different* staff members
(one proposes it, a different one has to approve it before anything
happens). Think of it like a bank: you can see your balance and ask for
a transfer, but you can't edit the bank's own ledger yourself. That's
what keeps the money safe.

## Overview — the front page

This is the first thing Amina sees when she logs in. It's a quick
summary, not a full list.

- A big number at the top: **"2 waqfs under Birr's trusteeship"** —
  she has two funds in total.
- A few boxes below it showing money, kept **separate by fund type and
  currency** rather than added together. For example:
  - "Declared corpus — Investment (USD): $50,000" (the Scholarship Waqf)
  - "Raised (USD): $12,000" (donations toward the Clean Water Project)
  - "Distributed via Investment funds (USD): $3,200" (scholarships
    already paid out)

  Why keep these separate instead of one big total? Because they're not
  really the same kind of money. The $50,000 invested capital is meant
  to stay untouched forever — only its profit gets spent. Adding it to
  the $12,000 in one-time donations would make it look like there's
  more "spendable" money than there really is.
- A simple chart showing how many of her funds are in each status (e.g.
  "1 active, 1 pending review").
- A short list of her foundations, with a link to see the full
  breakdown, and a button to add another waqf fund.

If Amina hadn't set up any waqf yet, this page would just say "Welcome"
and invite her to get started — not a scary "0" that looks like
something's wrong.

## Portfolio — where everything lives

This is the real, searchable list: every Foundation Amina has, and
every waqf fund under each one. She can search by name, and clicking
any fund opens that **fund's own page** — this is where almost all the
detail is.

Depending on the type of fund, that page can show up to eleven
sections. Here's what each one means, in plain terms, using Amina's two
funds as examples:

| Section | In plain English | Can Amina do anything here? |
|---|---|---|
| **Lifecycle** *(Clean Water Project only)* | A checklist of every stage a project waqf goes through, from "just started" to "long-term wrap-up." Shows Amina exactly where her project currently stands. | No, just for viewing |
| **Project Progress** *(Clean Water Project only)* | The actual milestones — e.g. "Well #1: Drilled ✅ — cost $2,100 of the $2,000 budgeted," with photos/evidence attached. | No, just for viewing |
| **Contributions** | How much has been donated so far, and the history of who gave what and when. | **Yes** — she can add more money here (card, local bank transfer, or stablecoin) |
| **Causes** | The specific purposes her fund supports — e.g. "Scholarships" or "Clean Water Access" — picked from Birr's standard list, and how much money is earmarked for each. | **Yes** — she can pick, unpick, and set the amount for each cause herself, no approval needed |
| **Assets** | Any physical property backing the fund (e.g. a building). | No — only Birr staff register or sell an asset |
| **Investments** *(Scholarship Waqf only)* | What the $50,000 is actually invested in, and whether it's been checked for Shariah compliance. | No, just for viewing |
| **Proceeds** *(Scholarship Waqf only)* | The profit the investment has earned so far, as logged by Birr's staff. | No, just for viewing |
| **Distributions** | A summary of payouts already made, grouped by cause (e.g. "$3,200 paid out for Scholarships so far") — not a list of who received what. | No, just for viewing |
| **Beneficiaries** | How many people are benefiting, and what the eligibility rules are — again, just a count, never names. | **Yes** — she can nominate someone she thinks should be considered, one at a time or in bulk, for Birr staff to review |
| **Requests** | A form to formally ask Birr for something — see below. | **Yes** — this is the main "ask" button on the whole dashboard |
| **Governance Activity** | A log of decisions Birr staff have already finished making about this fund (approved or rejected) — nothing still "in progress" shows up here. | No, just for viewing |
| **Financial Report** | A report Amina can generate any time, and export as a PDF, e.g. for her own board meeting. | **Yes** — she can generate this whenever she wants |

Two things worth knowing about the **Beneficiaries** section: Amina
never sees the actual names or personal details of the people her fund
helps — only totals and summaries. That's standard practice for
charities everywhere: donors don't get to see a private list of
recipients. The same privacy rule applies to **Distributions** — she
sees "how much went to Scholarships," never "who got paid."

### Requests — how Amina asks Birr for something {#requests-the-portals-request-half}

Say Amina wants Birr to approve a bigger scholarship payout this term,
or wants to flag that a piece of property should be sold. She fills in
a **Request** — picking the type (distribution approval, investment
change, beneficiary-criteria change, or asset disposal), the relevant
cause, and a description.

This is *not* the same as approving it herself — think of it exactly
like submitting a maintenance request to your landlord. It goes into a
queue for Birr's staff to review. If they agree, *they* create the real
approval, and — same rule as everywhere else — one staff member
proposes it and a *different* one has to sign off before anything
actually happens.

## Messages — her inbox with Birr

A simple inbox: one row per Foundation she has a conversation with,
most recent message first. If Amina only has one Foundation, this is
just one thread. Unread messages show a small dot, and the same unread
count shows up next to "Messages" in the sidebar so she never misses
one. Clicking a conversation takes her to that Foundation's page, where
the actual chat is.

## Impact — what her generosity actually achieved

This is the feel-good page. Every time Birr's staff log an update about
how a cause is progressing — e.g. *"Scholarships, Term 1 2026: 8
students funded this term, all currently enrolled"* — it shows up here,
pulled together across **all** of Amina's funds in one place, so she
doesn't have to check each fund separately. She can search by fund,
cause, or keyword. This page is read-only — it's Birr reporting back to
her, not the other way around.

## Team — who else can use the account

If Amina isn't running Hope for Kids Foundation alone, this is where
she manages who else has access. There are three levels:

- **Viewer** — can look around, that's it.
- **Requester** — can also do everything a Viewer can, plus pick/unpick
  causes and submit Requests.
- **Primary contact** — full access, including inviting new people or
  removing them. (Amina, as the founder, starts out as this.)

Only a Primary contact sees the "Invite someone" button — everyone else
just doesn't see it, since they wouldn't be allowed to use it anyway.
Inviting a colleague sends them an email with a link to join; if the
email can't be sent automatically, Amina gets a copy of that link to
send herself (by WhatsApp, for example) — that's exactly the situation
shown in the second screenshot earlier in this conversation, where the
system says *"Couldn't send the email automatically — copy this link
and send it yourself."* Pending invitations that haven't been accepted
yet are listed separately, and Amina can cancel one if she invited the
wrong person.

## Activity — her own history book

A running, newest-first log of everything Amina and her team have
actually done: adding money, picking causes, signing the deed, inviting
a teammate, and so on. Think of it as a bank statement for *actions*,
not money.

This is different from the **Governance Activity** section inside each
fund — that one shows decisions *Birr's staff* made about that specific
fund. This **Activity** page shows *Amina's own team's* actions, across
everything she owns, in one place. It exists because every action was
always being quietly recorded behind the scenes for audit purposes —
this page just finally shows that record back to her.

## Account & security — keeping her own login safe

Found by clicking Amina's name at the bottom of the sidebar. Two
things live here:

- **Two-factor authentication (2FA)** — an optional extra layer of
  security. If she turns it on, signing in also asks for a 6-digit code
  from an app like Google Authenticator, in addition to her password.
  She scans a QR code once to set it up, and gets a set of backup codes
  to save somewhere safe in case she ever loses her phone. (This is
  optional for founders like Amina — Birr's own staff are required to
  use it, since they can actually move money.)
- **Notification preferences** — a set of toggles for four kinds of
  updates (governance & waqf status, money movement, team & causes,
  messages), each one switchable independently for email and WhatsApp.
  Everything always shows up in the bell icon at the top of the
  dashboard regardless of these settings — this just controls what also
  gets sent to her inbox or phone.
