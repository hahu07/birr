Islam encourages several kinds of giving. They are easy to mix up, so here is how they differ.

## Zakat

An obligation for those who meet the conditions. It is calculated on eligible wealth and paid to specific categories of recipients. It is spent, not preserved.

## Sadaqah

Voluntary giving, in any amount, at any time. Like zakat, the gift is usually spent on the need it meets.

## Waqf

A lasting endowment. The asset is kept and its benefit continues to flow. A waqf is a good fit when you want your giving to outlast a single year or a single lifetime.

## Which should you choose?

They are not alternatives. Many givers do all three. If your goal is a lasting source of support for a cause, a waqf is the tool built for it — see [what a waqf is](/blog/what-is-a-waqf).

*Questions about whether a particular gift counts as zakat or sadaqah should go to a qualified scholar; this guide is general information only.*
